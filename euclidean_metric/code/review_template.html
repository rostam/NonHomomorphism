The Euclidean α-profile metric

4-agent discussion (theory P, computation Q, data analysis R, literature S), two rounds · 2026-10-26 · α(T,G) = |T,G| = min #edges deleted from T so that T → G.
Interactive 3D view (PCA axes) → 3D view with graphs as axes → Graph zoo (136 graphs) → Paper draft (PDF) → LaTeX source →

Summary. The profile φ(G) = (α(T,G)/e(T))T gives an exact Euclidean metric E on homomorphism classes.

  • It reverses the homomorphism order. The main principal axis is the height in that order.
  • It reads the clique number ω and the odd girth exactly.
  • It cannot see χ: no finite family of test graphs can. A source-side profile ψ, equal to the FJT quantity 1 − s(T,G), adds χ.
  • Much of the theory is in Engström–Färnqvist–Jonsson–Thapper (2015) under the name "separation".
  • New here: the Euclidean embedding, an ω-strengthening of the clique lower bound, the blindness result, the link to γ, and the computations.

1. Definition

φ(G) = ( wT · α(T,G) / e(T) )T ∈ 𝔉     E(G,H) = ‖φ(G) − φ(H)‖2
  • Test family 𝔉: the 85 cores (all cores on ≤ 8 vertices, plus C9, C11, Petersen, Grötzsch, Icosahedron).
  • Weights: χ-balanced, wT = 1/√Nχ(T), so each chromatic class of tests carries equal total squared weight.
  • G is the target. That is why φ is constant on homomorphism classes: α(2K4,K3) = 2 ≠ 1 = α(K4,K3), but every α(T,·) agrees on 2K4 and K4.
  • EFJT L2.6Order embedding: G → H ⇒ φ(G) ≥ φ(H) coordinatewise. The converse holds when 𝔉 contains a graph equivalent to G (P).

2. Shape of the space

  • One dominant axis. PC1 carries 92% of the variance and is the height ‖φ‖1. It never decreases along any of the 615,167 comparable pairs among the 1276 cores (R).
  • EFJT P2.4Clique coordinates depend only on ω: α(Kn,G) = C(n,2) − t(n, min(n,ω(G))). Moreover supT φT(G) = 1/ω(G) (P).
  • EFJT P2.5Odd-cycle coordinates take only the values 0 and 1/(2k+1), so they read the odd girth exactly. Odd cycles converge to K2.
  • Clusters follow ω, not χ. Nearest neighbours share ω 96% of the time and χ 81% (R).
  • NewBounded tests can't see χ. If every test has ≤ m vertices and G has odd girth > m, then φ(G) = φ(K2) (P). Q's example: the generalized Mycielskian μ5(C11), with 67 vertices and χ = 4, has exactly the profile of K2 on a 12-test basis.

3. Complete graphs, circular cliques and generalized Petersen graphs

The complete graphs form a straight chain K8 → K7 → … → K3 along the main axis, then jump to K2 at the triangle-free end, where the odd cycles accumulate.

Circular cliques: a Farey staircase

The circular cliques Kp/q form a chain in the homomorphism order. It contains the complete graphs Kn/1 and the odd cycles K(2k+1)/k. In profile space this chain is a staircase:

  • 13 of the 24 are themselves test graphs: K2…K8, C5, C7, C9, C11, K7/2 and K8/3.
  • Each of the other 11 lands exactly on the largest circular clique below it whose numerator is ≤ 8. For example, K13/6 lands on K2, K12/5 on C7, K11/4 on K8/3, and K11/3 on K7/2.
  • K10/3 has χ = 4 but the same profile as K3.
  • NewWhy. χc of a graph on ≤ m vertices is a fraction with numerator ≤ m, and X → Kp/q iff χc(X) ≤ p/q (Vince 1988; Zhu 2001). So tests with ≤ m vertices give φ(Kp/q) = φ(K⌊p/q⌋m), where ⌊r⌋m is the largest fraction ≤ r with numerator ≤ m.

Generalized Petersen graphs

Every GP(n,k) with n ≤ 12 lands exactly on one of seven cores: K2, K3, C5, C7, C9, C11 or Petersen. For 11 of them this is not homomorphic equivalence. They are 3-chromatic graphs of odd girth 5 or 7 that do not map to C5 or C7 (checked by exhaustive search), yet no test with ≤ 12 vertices tells them apart from the cycle. This is the blindness result in action.

4. Seeing χ: the source-side profile ψ

  • Why it's needed. φ records what maps into G, while χ is about where G maps to. The naive α(G,T)/e(G) is not class-invariant (K3 vs Kn,n,n).
  • EFJT T2.1, P5.4Definition (P). ψT(G) = max over edge weights of αw(G,T)/‖w‖, which equals 1 − s(T,G) (FJT) and 1 − 1/χT(G).
  • Properties. ψT is class-invariant and order-preserving. ψT(G) = 0 iff G → T, so ψKk(G) > 0 iff χ(G) > k.
  • Lattice operations. ψ(G⊔H) = max. ψ(G×H) ≤ min, and Shitov's counterexample to Hedetniemi shows this can be strict even on the support.
  • Together: φ reads ω and ψ reads χ, so the gap χ − ω is what panel (b) of the summary figure shows.

6. Recommendations

  • Default metric: all cores as tests with χ-balanced weights. On the 1276 cores it correlates .92 with dist and .83 with d*, merges no pairs, and is very stable (Spearman ρ ≥ .9995 when any one test is removed).
  • Cheap variant: the 85 tests, which gives ρ = .991 with the default but merges about 5% of pairs.
  • Avoid: the 12-test basis alone (only 16 classes on the 1276 cores) and 1/e(T) weights.
  • Good for: a height function on the hom order; exact ω and odd girth; one embedding carrying three distances; a geometric picture of χ − ω (with ψ).
  • Not good for: χ without ψ; compact invariants; graph-ML features on large graphs, where most coordinates are 0.

7. Open questions

  1. openHow does the minimum size of a separating test family grow? For n ≤ 9 it lies in [253, 311].

  3. openDoes the unweighted source-side profile equal the weighted ψ for all G and T?
